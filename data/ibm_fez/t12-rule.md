# Test 12: the rule, fixed before the run

Written 17 October 2026, before any job was built or submitted. Author: Anish Patel. This file states the design, the analysis and the verdict rule for test 12 on ibm_fez qubit 143. Its SHA-256 is posted publicly as an issue before the job is submitted, together with the digests of the desk model's predictions written before this rule, and the file is published unchanged after the run. The verdict rule is checked on the desk, on simulated traces of 143, before the hash and again by the runner after it; both checks are published with the run.

## The question

Test 11 (17 October 2026) read qubit 143's fringe at the antiphase wait on a 0.6 s clock through two monitor jobs: within an execution span nothing moved above the design's floor in phase or in weight; between spans the weight sat at different levels, resolved at many times their errors; and the rule's own boundary count had no pair to test, because on ibm_fez every span boundary fell inside a repeat. A desk model fitted to tests 7 to 11, Sandhi, describes the records with five rates and wrote its bets before this rule; the sharpest of them concerns a monitor on 143: that the weight's level differs between adjacent spans at nearly every boundary, that it does not switch within a span on this clock, and that the phase does not switch at all above the floor.

On ibm_fez, in one short monitor job on 143 at the antiphase wait, 123 repeats on a 0.6 s clock across two span boundaries: does the weight's level differ between adjacent spans at the record's own resolution; does the weight or the phase switch within a span; and what does the record say at each of the model's bets?

## The code

Manacitra at the v0.8.0 release (or v0.7.0, `b782d00238b7df40e1fbfd4da674968cf653c8d7`, if 0.8.0 is not installed; the commit used is recorded), installed unedited, for the single-qubit circuit form (`part_b_circuit(1, wait_dt, "R")`, `even_wait`, `to_backend`). The monitor circuits, the per-repeat phase and weight, the switch tests and the verdicts are test 11's run script (`monitor_t11.py`, published with test 11's data) with the shot count, the repeat count and the boundary-pair rule below changed, published with this run's data. **The result's execution metadata (`execution_spans`, and everything else under the result's `metadata`) is saved with the counts at fetch; a job whose metadata cannot be saved is recorded as such and its spans are retrieved again before analysis.**

## The run: one job on ibm_fez, qubit 143

Settings: dynamical decoupling, gate twirling and measurement twirling all off; no readout error mitigation. Qubit 143 alone; every other qubit idle in |0⟩. One job, submitted once; a failed or cancelled job is recorded and not resubmitted, and the test reports no verdict.

- **The wait:** 5.6 µs, test 11's antiphase wait, fixed; the scheduled wait is recorded and compared with the requested one.
- **One repeat** is two circuits, X (H, delay, H, measure) then Y (the same with S† before the final H), each **500 shots**.
- **The job:** the two readout circuits (measure; X then measure), then **123 repeats** in the same order every time, then the two readout circuits again: **250 circuits**, each 500 shots, submitted in that order and not interleaved. At the provider's span pitch of about 100 positions seen on this processor (101, 100, 99 in test 11), the job crosses two span boundaries with at least twenty whole repeats on each side of each; the actual spans are whatever the execution record says.
- **Structural checks before submission, on the circuits as built and again on the backend's snapshot:** every instruction acts on 143; 143 carries exactly the circuit's gate sequence with its scheduled wait; the exact noiseless P(1) is 0 for X, 0.5 for Y, 0 and 1 for the readout circuits; the 250 positions are in the rule's order. Any failure stops the run.
- **The estimate and the allowance:** the account's remaining allowance is read and recorded with the UTC time before the job; the estimate is test 11's job 2 measured rate per shot times 125,000 shots, with IBM's formula beside; the job is submitted only if its estimate with the half-again margin is within the remaining allowance. The decision is recorded before the job is built. The backend list the account exposes at build is recorded.

### When

ibm_fez's published stamps for the previous 48 hours are read before building; no job is submitted inside the computed window or while the day's cycle is in progress, as test 7's rule states; if the 48 hours show no pattern or the interval lies outside 20 to 30 hours, that is recorded and the window does not apply. The stamp at build, at submission, every 5 minutes, and after the fetch is recorded.

## The analysis

1. **The midpoint c** is the mean of the four readout circuits' P(1).
2. **Per repeat k = 1, ..., 123:** P_X,k and P_Y,k; the vector v_k = (P_X,k − c, P_Y,k − c); its angle θ_k, its length r_k; the angle's standard error s_k = σ_k / r_k with σ_k² = (P_X(1 − P_X) + P_Y(1 − P_Y)) / (2 × 500), floored at 0.02 rad; the length's standard error σ_k.
3. **Usable:** the median r_k over the job at least 0.08.
4. **The spans:** from the result's execution metadata, each position belongs to the span the provider's record places it in, whatever its number; each repeat is assigned the span that ran both of its circuits; a repeat whose two circuits ran in different spans is **a straddling repeat**, named, excluded from the within-span counts, its θ_k and r_k still reported.
5. **The boundary pair** at each span boundary is the last whole repeat before it and the first whole repeat after it, whether or not a straddling repeat lies between them (test 11's boundary table, now the rule); where two straddling repeats lie at one boundary, the pair spans both. The boundary's gap is the provider's gap between the spans.
6. **The unwrapped phase and a phase switch** as test 10's rule: a switch between the two repeats of a within-span pair or a boundary pair where the wrapped |Δθ| exceeds the larger of 0.3 rad and 5 √(s_k² + s_(k+1)²). At 5.6 µs a radian is 28.4 kHz; on 143 at antiphase (r about 0.13 to 0.17) and 500 shots the five-standard-error term, about 1.0 rad, is the floor: **a common change of both lines' frequency is seen at about 30 kHz and above; a change of the dominant line is a half turn.** The floor is named as a limit of the design.
7. **The weight per repeat:** w_k = (1 − r_k / R0) / 2 for a repeat whose θ_k is within π/2 of the job's median phase (the median taken on one sheet of the circle, test 11's ruling), w_k = (1 + r_k / R0) / 2 for a repeat a half turn away, with standard error σ_k / (2 R0). **R0 = 0.4749**, test 11's job 1 P fit at 5.6 µs (A 0.495, τ′ 132.5 µs), fixed here, since no shuffled job runs today; the branch assignment follows from test 11's weight below one half, and an exchange is recorded if the job's weight reads above one half.
8. **A weight switch** between the two repeats of a within-span pair or a boundary pair where |Δw| exceeds the larger of 0.10 and 5 √(the sum of the two squared standard errors). At 500 shots the five-standard-error term is about 0.16, which is the pair floor the design carries and is named as a limit. **A limit of the branch rule:** a common change of both lines' frequency larger than about a quarter turn (45 kHz) moves repeats across the branch line and the weight series is unreliable from that pair on; so if a phase switch occurs whose size is not within 0.6 rad of a half turn, the weight and level verdicts are still computed and reported with that note, and the phase verdict is the verdict of record for that job. A half-turn phase switch is a change of the dominant line, which is a weight event by construction, and both verdicts stand.
9. **The weight per span:** the mean of w_k over the span's whole repeats, with the larger of the propagated error and the scatter over √n. **A level change at a span boundary** is a difference of adjacent span means exceeding five times its combined standard error (about 0.03 at this shot count with twenty or more repeats per span, named as the resolution). The span means and their errors are reported with the number of whole repeats in each. A weight switch within a span also changes the span means on either side, so the level words are read beside the within-span counts, never alone.
10. **Within each span:** the scatter of w_k about the span mean (χ² per degree of freedom) and a weighted straight-line slope of w_k against k, and the same for θ_k.

## The verdicts

**The phase:** QUIET (no phase switch within a span or at a boundary pair); SWITCHES AT BOUNDARIES ONLY; SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS. **The weight, by pairs:** WEIGHT QUIET; WEIGHT SWITCHES AT BOUNDARIES ONLY; WEIGHT SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS. **The weight, by levels:** LEVELS DIFFER AT EVERY BOUNDARY (a level change at each span boundary the job crossed); LEVELS DIFFER AT SOME (at least one but not every); LEVELS AGREE (none). A job in which the monitor is not usable is NOT USABLE with its numbers and gives no verdict.

**The counts of the run,** as the model's bets are written: the number of span boundaries the job crossed; the number of level changes at them; the number of weight switches and phase switches at the boundary pairs and within spans; the within-span time; the level changes' sizes with their errors.

## The model's bets, fixed before this rule, and what counts against each

The desk model Sandhi's predictions file (`predictions-model-01-v3.md` and `.json`, digests posted with this rule's hash) gives, from its monitor class at the record's own resolution, a probability of 0.95 (interval 0.64 to 1.00) that the weight's level differs across any one monitor boundary, so about 0.997 that it differs at at least one of two; its predictive count for the boundaries this job crosses is taken from the file's declared-trial-count table by the runner and reported; **a job in which the levels agree at every boundary counts against the model's monitor class.** From its within-span weight rate (median 0.0014 per qubit-second, interval 10⁻⁵ to 0.011) the probability of at least one within-span weight switch in about 70 s is about 0.09 at the median; from its phase channels the probability of any phase switch is below 0.01 and the predictive count is 0; a phase switch of any size, within a span or at a boundary, counts against those channels. The model's file states its monitor floor as about 18 to 23 kHz at 1,000 shots; this job's floor is about 30 kHz at 500 shots, and the model's bets are read at the record's own floor as its file says. Each count of the run is set beside the model's number; nothing here changes the rule or the model after the data.

## Reported beside the verdicts, not used in them

1. **The execution record:** the spans, their start and stop, their positions, the gaps, the repeat period per span, the straddling repeats, any position run out of submitted order.
2. **The boundary table:** at each boundary pair, the wrapped phase difference and the weight difference with their standard errors, beside the same differences across a within-span pair drawn at the middle of each span (the control).
3. **The θ_k, r_k and w_k series in full**, the levels found and their separation in radians.
4. **The weight per span** beside test 11's (job 2: 0.340, 0.392, 0.360; job 3: 0.323, 0.350, 0.237; job 1 by A26's method: 0.496, 0.433, 0.448), with the hours between.
5. **Readout** from the in-job readout circuits beside the published value at the stamp in force.
6. **The stamp** at build, at submission, every 5 minutes and after the fetch, beside test 11's (18:48:16 UTC, 17 October); the backend list; the estimate, the allowance reading and the decision.

The rule is not changed after the data, whatever they show.

## The desk check, before the hash

The analysis and verdicts are run on simulated jobs of 123 repeats with binomial shot noise at 500 shots, spans of positions 0 to 100, 101 to 200 and 201 to 249 (test 11's pitch; the straddling repeats fall at 50 and 100), on a qubit with test 7's fitted parameters for 143 (c 0.49; A 0.487; τ′ 128 µs; two lines 89.6 kHz apart; weight 0.355) at the 5.6 µs wait, 100 trials each: (1) steady: QUIET, WEIGHT QUIET and LEVELS AGREE at 95% or more, the median w_k within 0.05 of 0.355. (2) The weight stepping from 0.355 to 0.10 at repeat 25 and back at repeat 75, within spans: WEIGHT SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS at 95% or more, both switches at the planted repeats in 90% or more, the phase QUIET; the same with a step to 0.15, recorded. (3) The weight's level stepping from 0.355 to 0.305 at the first boundary and to 0.405 at the second (steps of 0.05 and 0.10, below the pair floor): LEVELS DIFFER AT EVERY BOUNDARY at 95% or more, WEIGHT QUIET by pairs in 90% or more (the pair test does not see a 0.10 step at this shot count, which is the point of the level test). (4) The weight flipping from 0.355 to 0.645 at the first boundary and back at the second: SWITCHES AT BOUNDARIES ONLY on the phase and WEIGHT SWITCHES AT BOUNDARIES ONLY by pairs at 95% or more, LEVELS DIFFER AT EVERY BOUNDARY at 95% or more, each phase step within 0.6 rad of a half turn in 90% or more. (5) Both lines stepping together by 50 kHz at repeat 25 and back at 75: SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS at 95% or more, both switches placed in 90% or more, the weight and level words recorded (a step of this size crosses the branch line, which is the limit named in step 8); the same with 30 kHz and 12 kHz steps, recorded. (6) The weight drawn fresh per shot at 0.5: NOT USABLE or QUIET, recorded. The thresholds (0.3 rad, 0.10 in weight, five standard errors, the 0.08 floor on r, the 0.02 rad floor on s) are kept only if every required rate is met; a change before the hash is recorded in this section with its reason.

Mantri's check before the hash (17 October, 100 trials per case, binomial noise and exponential decay only, which is a limit of the check): every required rate met. Case 1: QUIET, WEIGHT QUIET and LEVELS AGREE 100 of 100, the weight recovered within 0.05 in 100; the measured phase floor 1.13 rad, 32 kHz; the pair weight floor 0.164; span errors 0.0037, 0.0033 and 0.0054 on 49, 49 and 23 whole repeats. Case 2: WEIGHT SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS 100 of 100 with both switches at the planted repeats in 100 (the step to 0.15, which sits just above the pair floor, 98 of 100 with both placed in 81, recorded). Case 3: LEVELS DIFFER AT EVERY BOUNDARY 100 of 100 with the pairs WEIGHT QUIET in 98. Case 4: SWITCHES AT BOUNDARIES ONLY on both channels and LEVELS DIFFER AT EVERY BOUNDARY 100 of 100, the two phase steps each within 0.6 rad of a half turn in 97. Case 5: SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS 100 of 100 with both placed in 95; the weight read WEIGHT SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS in 100 and the levels AGREE in 89, the branch limit of step 8 as expected; a 30 kHz step read SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS in 61 of 100 (the floor), a 12 kHz step QUIET in 100. Case 6: NOT USABLE (median r 0.026). One desk-check case was changed before the hash and is recorded: case 2's planted step was first written as a step to 0.15 and found both switches in 86 of 100, below the 90 required, because 0.205 is only a quarter above the pair floor at this shot count; the case was moved to a step to 0.10 and the 0.15 step kept as a recorded sensitivity. No threshold of the rule changed.
