# What it has shown, and what it has not

The findings come from runs on 5 to 13 October 2026. The counts are in `data/`. Every statistic listed in `tests/expected_fields.json` is recomputed from the archived inputs: the raw counts where the release includes them; for the Kickoff 29 settling runs and some Rigetti comparisons, the archived probability or level tables, whose counts are not in this release; for Kickoff 43's chain search, its map, the published figures and the coupling map; and, for elapsed times, the archived timestamps, and compared at 10⁻⁶, except the seeded resampling fields: the intervals that also resample shots (Kickoff 33's, and Kickoffs 43 to 48's bootstraps with their recorded seeds), at 10⁻³, and Kickoff 42's bootstrap SDs, at 10⁻⁶ under numpy 2.5 or later (each marked in `tests/expected_fields.json`). Fields that cannot be recomputed from this release are named, with reasons, in `tests/excluded_fields.json`, and so are the 117 bootstrap fields of Kickoffs 43 to 48 in families that move by more than 10⁻³ with the platform's random stream (Kickoff 48's measured over 200 other seeds), and five ranks and rank correlations whose ties the platform's sort orders, none of them in the text. Both lists are checked by `pytest tests/test_reproduction.py tests/test_field_inventory.py` (from a clone, after `pip install -e ".[dev,aer]"`, which gives `pytest`). Every measured statistic in the README and on this page is checked by a test; times, ranges and counts are taken from the data files and listed in `tests/test_readme_numbers.py`.

1. <a id="finding-1"></a>**Each pair keeps its own share, and the share repeats** (Kickoff 31; ibm_fez at 12:37 UTC, ibm_kingston at 12:38 UTC; 27 pairs each). Within one job, the map from one half of the circuits correlates with the map from the other half at r = 0.87 (ibm_fez) and 0.90 (ibm_kingston). It carries over to a second circuit with a different gap and its own reference: r = 0.82 and 0.89. The published score does not account for it (r = −0.18 and −0.05). Verdict on both: DIAGNOSTIC.
2. <a id="finding-2"></a>**The map exists on a second vendor's chip, within one job** (Kickoff 34b; Rigetti Cepheus-1-108Q, through Open Quantum, 5 October 2026, main job 22:15 to 22:29 UTC). A screening run chose 27 pairs, and 22 of them were working pairs in the main job. Kickoff 37 later found that the five pairs excluded here read low only under this program: under the 53-pair screen program they read 0.70 to 0.84. Kickoff 40 later ran the same 27 named pairs on this processor through a route that records placement, and found that this job's pair levels do not match the pinned levels (r = −0.17 on the 20 shared working pairs). The pair names in this job cannot be read as locations, since the route records none and the pinned run's levels do not match them; the within-job finding stands as measured. Within that job, each pair's kept share repeated (the split halves correlate at r = 0.985) and carried over from circuit A to circuit B (r = 0.971). Verdict: MAP PRESENT. Two pairs whose offset circuits collapsed, 13-14 and 101-102, inflate these Pearson figures. Without them, on 20 pairs, the repeat check is 0.84 and the carry-over 0.79, and the rule still gives MAP PRESENT; this 20-pair figure was computed after seeing the data. (The leave-one-out check, fixed before the run according to the author's dated records, which are not part of this release, drops 101-102 alone and also gives MAP PRESENT.) The chip publishes no per-pair figures, so the test cannot say whether the map adds anything beyond them.
3. <a id="finding-3"></a>**The gap between the best and worst pairs persisted when their neighbours were idle** (Kickoff 32; ibm_fez at 13:17 UTC, ibm_kingston at 13:23 UTC). The six pairs that kept the most and the six that kept the least were run again, once with all 27 pairs active and once with no two active pairs coupled. The gap between the two groups did not close when the neighbours were idle: the closed fraction was 0.01 on ibm_fez (90% interval −0.21 to 0.21) and 0.03 on ibm_kingston (−0.09 to 0.16). The result rules out crowding by the tested neighbours as the cause; it does not separate the two-qubit gate from single-qubit control, readout or drift. The sealed rule's verdict on both is named THE GATE; here that name means "not explained by the tested neighbours".
4. <a id="finding-4"></a>**On ibm_fez, choosing pairs by the map gave 35.6% less error on unrelated random circuits than choosing by the published error rates** (Kickoff 33; 15:20 UTC). Gain in fidelity G = +0.0012, 90% interval +0.0004 to +0.0021; verdict USEFUL. **On ibm_kingston (15:23 UTC), 16% less, not statistically settled**: G = +0.0004 (+0.0001 to +0.0008), but the map's partial correlation with the workload, after removing the published score, was 0.29 (p = 0.073), short of the rule's 0.3 and p < 0.05. Verdict: NOT SETTLED. Beside the verdict, as the rule fixed before the run specified for reference: choosing by the plain level before the job gained less than choosing by the kept share on both chips (G = +0.0004 on ibm_fez and +0.0001 on ibm_kingston, against +0.0012 and +0.0004); and a map measured in the same job as the workload, a ceiling by the rule's wording, did no better than the published figures on ibm_fez (G = −0.0003, 90% interval −0.0010 to +0.0003) and gained +0.0004 on ibm_kingston. The verdict is about the map a user would have in hand before the job; the same-day figure says the 35.6% depends on which eight pairs a map picks, and two maps a few hours apart (r = 0.80 between them) did not pick the same eight. To recompute the ibm_fez figure from the raw counts: the prior map is Kickoff 32's dense condition (`k32-isolation.json`, not `k31-map.json`), each random circuit's two runs are pooled per pair (16,000 shots) before the fidelity, the fidelity is the squared Hellinger overlap with each circuit's ideal on uncorrected counts, and the published pick uses the x recorded at submission; `python examples/03_pick_pairs.py` makes both picks, and [`data/README.md`](../data/README.md) gives the details.
5. <a id="finding-5"></a>**The rule can say no** (Kickoff 36, a simulation of the noise model Cirq publishes for Google's willow_pink, run on a laptop; not a measurement of any device). When every difference between pairs came from the published figures, the rule called the map NOISE: those differences were too small to repeat above shot noise. When a per-pair error was planted outside the published figures, the rule found it (DIAGNOSTIC).
6. <a id="finding-6"></a>**On the Rigetti processor, a pair's level depends on the program that measures it, not on the hour** (Kickoff 37; Rigetti Cepheus-1-108Q, through Open Quantum, 6 October 2026; first wave 00:45 to 02:22 UTC, second wave 11:31 to 11:35 UTC). Two programs from Kickoff 34b were sent again, unchanged: the main job's 27-pair program and the 53-pair screen, which contains the same 27 pairs. Each program agreed with itself: r = 0.997 to 0.998 minutes apart, 0.97 (27-pair) and 0.76 (53-pair) across about 9 hours, and 0.99 and 0.98 against its own Kickoff 34b run the day before. The two programs did not agree with each other on the 27 pairs they share: r = 0.07 in the first wave and −0.25 in the second, when all three tasks ran within 3 minutes. Verdict, by a rule fixed before the run according to the author's dated records: PLACEMENT, read as "the levels depend on the program". Kickoff 38 (6 October 2026, 13:42 to 13:46 UTC) then sent a third program that measures the 53-pair program's 106 qubits but runs only the 27-pair program's gates: it read like the 27-pair program (r = 0.99) and not like the 53-pair one (r = −0.15), so what moves the levels is the program's gate graph, not which qubits it measures (verdict ACTIVITY). Kickoff 40 then ran the same named pairs on the same processor through Amazon Braket, where the compiled program returned with each task shows where each pair ran. The levels of Kickoff 37's two programs do not match the pinned levels of the same named pairs (r = −0.18 and 0.24). Open Quantum records no placement, so where its pairs ran is not measured; the reading that fits is that the compiler placed them by the program's gate graph, which is also what Kickoff 38's result points to. An alternative, that the same physical pairs respond differently to differently compiled programs (Kickoff 42 shows responses are program-specific), is not excluded by these runs. The levels depended on the program; on the reading above, because the program's gates decided where the pairs went. The second wave was planned two hours after the first and ran nine hours after it, so the two-hour question was not tested.
7. <a id="finding-7"></a>**On ibm_fez, a map of the whole chip held for two days** (Kickoff 35; all 176 coupled pairs in 3 rounds, one job a day on 5, 6 and 7 October 2026 at 17:02, 14:56 and 14:21 UTC, across two daily recalibrations). The map from Day 3 correlates with Day 1 at r = 0.86 (0.89 after correcting for each day's own repeatability), and 9 of the 18 worst pairs on Day 1 were still among the 18 worst on Day 3, exactly the rule's bar of 50%. Verdict: HOLDS, under the rule fixed before the run and under its amendment. The decile condition is met with no margin, and a repeat could as easily have read PARTIAL; the correlations are the solid part. Four pairs that IBM lists at a two-qubit error of 1.0 ran, and three of them carry the most extreme values on the chip; without all four the corrected correlation from Day 1 to Day 3 is 0.74. On Day 2 both pairs on one qubit fell sharply and recovered on Day 3, while k fell by 3.5 and 4.1 on Day 2 and came back by 4.7 and 7.6 on Day 3, and x changed by at most 16%. IBM's published score is steadier day to day than the map (r = 0.99) and does not predict it (r between 0.07 and −0.07).

10. <a id="finding-10"></a>**On the Rigetti processor, a pair's response to a particular program repeats a day later, and a smooth shift of the offset does not describe it** (Kickoff 42; the same pairs, pinned, circuit A at nine offsets and circuit B at nine, 6 October 2026 at 17:46 UTC and 7 October at 16:17 UTC). The ideal curve shifted and scaled fits fewer than one pair in ten within twice the shot noise, and the fitted shift does not explain the kept share (r = +0.24). What repeats is each pair's pattern over the 18 programs: pooled r = 0.85 a day later, median per-pair r = 0.98, with two pairs at 0.04. Pair 94-95 has a dip at exactly the designed offset rather than a moved peak; pair 11-12 read about 0.3 higher on the same seven programs on both days, and 72-73 read 0.21 higher on the first day and 0.11 on the second. The fitted shifts also repeat once the pairs whose fit ran to the grid's edge are set aside (r = 0.72 for A, 0.90 for B; HOLDS under the amended rule, PARTIAL under the original). This is the evidence behind the sentence that a map is a map for one family of circuits.
11. <a id="finding-11"></a>**On ibm_fez, reuse circuits placed by the map scored higher than Qiskit's calibration placement, mostly because the map's chains avoided one qubit** (Kickoff 43; ibm_fez, 10 October 2026: the map at 04:13 UTC, the run from 04:17 to 04:54 UTC). Four public circuits, XOR_5, BV_10, Mul_13 and Sym_9, are among those QR-Map tested (Kim et al., ISCA 2025, doi [10.1145/3695053.3731020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3695053.3731020)), which names RevLib (Wille et al., ISMVL 2008) and QASMBench (Li et al., ACM Transactions on Quantum Computing, 2023) as their sources. The files used are the CaQR repository's (Hua et al., ASPLOS 2023) for XOR_5, BV_10 and Sym_9, and QASMBench's for Mul_13. Their licences do not clearly allow redistribution, so this release keeps each file's source, commit and SHA-256, and `tools/fetch_reuse_circuits.py` fetches and checks them. Each circuit was compiled with qubit reuse (mid-circuit measurement and reset, by a reimplementation of QR-Map's Tapering written from the paper, not in this release) and without it, and each compiled circuit was placed on a chain of qubits twice: by the map's chain search, and by Qiskit's own layout from IBM's published calibration. F is the squared Hellinger overlap of a placement's pooled counts (100,000 shots) with the circuit's exact ideal output, and G is F on the map's chain minus F on Qiskit's. By the rule fixed before the run, according to the author's dated records, the verdict is USEFUL FOR REUSE: G_reuse = +0.2246, 90% interval +0.2227 to +0.2267. Without reuse, the same circuits gave G = +0.0182 (+0.0161 to +0.0204).

    By circuit, G_reuse was +0.743 for XOR_5, +0.288 for BV_10, +0.035 for Sym_9 and −0.167 for Mul_13, where Qiskit's placement did better. Without XOR_5 the mean is +0.052. The interval covers shot and copy noise only, not the spread between circuits, so the size of the mean is not a general effect size.

    The score was changed before the run, by Amendment A1 (fixed before the run, according to the author's dated records): a chain scores the gate-weighted sum of |1 − k| over its couplers, in place of the brief's 1 − k, because a k well above 1 marks a damaged pair, not a good one. Under the original rule, all eight chains would have gone through coupler 33-34 (k 4.65, plain level 0.52). It is the fix 0.2.0 made to `manacitra pick`.

    The map's own verdict that morning was NOT SETTLED. The map was built from circuit A alone, a design under which the rule cannot reach DIAGNOSTIC, because the carry-over to circuit B is not measured.

    The explanation came later, from finding 13. In the calibration placements of XOR_5 and BV_10, the two circuits with the largest gains, qubit 143 sat on a line that is never retired, between lines that are measured and reset; in Sym_9, 143's line was retired once, and in Mul_13, four times. The map's chains kept 143 out of all four circuits. Neither the map, which scores couplers, nor the published figures see what was wrong with 143. So the gain is not evidence that the map's coupler information chooses better placements for reuse circuits. This is a reading after the data.

    Kickoff 43's brief required a comparison against MERA-style placement, which places a circuit by each qubit's measured mid-circuit measurement error (Zhong et al., [arXiv:2511.10921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10921)), before any claim. That comparison has not been run, and this finding makes no claim that needs it.
12. <a id="finding-12"></a>**On ibm_fez, a reuse circuit collapsed on chains around qubits 143 and 144, and five explanations for it were tested and set aside** (Kickoffs 44, 45 and 46; ibm_fez, 10 October 2026, at 05:40, 05:57 and 07:07 UTC). XOR_5's reuse circuit, three lines of which two are retired, collapsed on 144-143-142 (F 0.087 in Kickoff 43, 0.090 in Kickoff 45 and 0.098 in Kickoff 46) and mirrored, on 142-143-144 (0.071); on 136-143-142 (0.091) and 136-143-144 (0.094); and on 143-144-145 (0.074). Not every chain containing 143 collapsed: 143-142-141 held (Kickoff 45, F 0.665), and 141-142-143 fell to the middle band (Kickoff 46, 0.467). The healthy chains held: 132-131-138 (0.830 in Kickoff 43, 0.829 in Kickoff 45 and 0.837 in Kickoff 46) and 125-124-123 (0.784). Without reuse, XOR_5 on a chain through the region, 124-123-136-143-142-141, scored 0.887 in Kickoff 43 and 0.868 in Kickoff 45.

    The five explanations, each with its verdict by a rule fixed before the run, according to the author's dated records:
    - **A single measure-and-reset** (Kickoff 44, on every qubit of the chip): NOT SUPPORTED. The qubits where Kickoff 43's reuse circuits failed did not retire worse than the ones where they succeeded: D = −0.034, 90% interval −0.039 to −0.028, below the margin of 0.02. A design error was found after the data: the control idled the waiting qubit in |1⟩, whose decay biases the measure low. Corrected with the published T1, after the data, D is −0.042. The verdict did not change.
    - **Repeated retirement** (Kickoff 45, one to three retirements on every qubit): NOT SUPPORTED, D = −0.018 (−0.021 to −0.014).
    - **Excess idle loss**, the excited state decaying faster than the published T1 predicts (Kickoff 45): SUPPORTED by its rule, D = +0.031 (+0.026 to +0.035). Beside it, a reading after the data: the difference is carried by qubit 141 (without it, D is +0.014, below the margin), and chain 143-142-141, which contains 141, held. Idle loss did not track the collapse.
    - **142's readout**: real but too small. 142's mid-circuit assignment error was 0.026 in Kickoff 44 and 0.049 in Kickoff 45, 8.3 and 15.6 times its published readout error. Errors of that size on the three results 142 records in chain 144-143-142 would lower F by a factor of about (1 − 0.049)³ = 0.86, while F fell to about a tenth of the healthy chain's (an estimate after the data). The error recovered after the calibration changed (0.004 in Kickoff 46, 1.3 times), and the collapse stayed.
    - **Disturbance from a neighbour's retirement** (Kickoff 46, a qubit waiting in superposition while a neighbour is measured and reset): NOT SUPPORTED, D = −0.003 (−0.018 to +0.011), below the margin of 0.05.

    Beside them, one reading of the placements, "143 as the middle line" (the collapse follows 143 waiting while both its neighbours on the chain are retired), was made after Kickoff 45's data. Kickoff 46's placements tested it, and it was set down: it needed 143-144-145 and 141-142-143 to hold, and the first collapsed while the second fell to the middle band.

    The runs did not all see the same published calibration. IBM's calibration stamp was 03:16:31 UTC when Kickoffs 43, 44 and 45 were built, and at Kickoff 45's fetch. At 06:19 UTC, when Kickoff 46 was checked, it read 05:52:36 UTC, four minutes before Kickoff 45 began running, and the CZ figures had changed. Kickoff 46 ran under 06:12:47 UTC, a later calibration than Kickoffs 43 to 45, although the published readout, T1 and T2 figures of the region's qubits were the same under all three stamps.
13. <a id="finding-13"></a>**Qubit 143 lost phase while it waited in superposition, IBM's echo-measured T2 does not show that kind of loss, and refocusing pulses restored the circuit** (Kickoff 47; ibm_fez, 10 October 2026, 10:54 to 10:57 UTC). On chain A, 144-143-142, XOR_5's reuse circuit collapsed again without refocusing (F 0.071, 90% interval 0.069 to 0.073), and scored 0.843 (0.838 to 0.847) with XY4 refocusing pulses (Maudsley, Journal of Magnetic Resonance 69, 488, 1986) inserted by Qiskit's [`PadDynamicalDecoupling`](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/api/qiskit/qiskit.transpiler.passes.PadDynamicalDecoupling) into every idle window of the chain long enough to hold the sequence: a gain of +0.772 (+0.767 to +0.777). By the rule fixed before the run, according to the author's dated records, H_phase is SUPPORTED. Beside it, chain J, 143-144-145, scored 0.875 without pulses and 0.863 with them, and the healthy chain C, 132-131-138, 0.833 and 0.784. Refocusing cost the healthy chain 0.049 and chain J 0.011.

    The wait itself (Part B). After 3.4 µs in superposition, 143's Ramsey loss, the rise in P(1) over no wait, was 0.223 (0.215 to 0.231); with one refocusing pulse halfway through the wait (a Hahn echo), the loss was 0.015 (0.012 to 0.018). By its rule, fixed before the run, the loss on 143 is REFOCUSABLE. In the circuit, as scheduled with ibm_fez's own instruction durations, 143 waited in three windows of 3.34 to 3.37 µs while its neighbours were measured and reset.

    IBM's QPU information guide reports T2 from a Hahn echo: "T₂ is reported from a Hahn echo sequence" ([quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/qpu-information](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/qpu-information), read 10 October 2026). That is an established finding about how the figure is made, cited, not tested here. An echo cancels a steady frequency offset, which would explain why 143's published T2 does not show this loss (a reading after the data).

    A fit, labelled as a fit: a single offset of 46.7 ± 2.7 kHz describes 143's Ramsey curve over 0 to 8 µs. The run leaves one point open: a steady offset and a frequency that switches slowly between offset values give the same curve over 8 µs, and an echo undoes both; this run does not separate them. It does rule out broad dephasing centred on zero, which cannot take a Ramsey P(1) above 0.5: 143's reached 0.80 at 8 µs (a reading after the data). The offset is a measurement of one qubit on one processor on one morning, not a statement about the quality of IBM's calibration. Manacitra does not rank machines.

    What changed between calibrations: 144's wait loss went from 0.15 under the 06:12 stamp (Kickoff 46, its P(1) after a wait of 3.37 µs in superposition) to 0.012 under the 09:56 stamp (here, the rise in P(1) over a wait of 3.4 µs), and chain J, which collapsed in Kickoff 46, held. 143's stayed (0.22 to 0.24 in Kickoff 46, 0.223 here). Across every chain since Kickoff 45, a chain collapsed when its waiting middle line had a large wait loss. That is a reading after the data, not a tested rule.

14. <a id="finding-14"></a>**On ibm_fez, a qubit's Ramsey loss at the circuit's own wait length predicted how a reuse-shaped circuit fared when that qubit waited in it** (Kickoff 48; ibm_fez, 11 October 2026, 02:13 to 02:14 UTC). The wait test of `manacitra.wait` (Amendment A18), built unedited from this repository at commit `f28642a` (v0.4.0), ran on five chains L1-M-L3, each without and with XY4 refocusing pulses: 144-143-142, 141-142-143, 125-124-123, 143-144-145 and 132-131-138, in four interleaved copies of 5,000 shots. In the wait test the middle line M waits in superposition while its neighbours are measured, reset and used again ([the method](method.md#the-wait-test)). In the same job, Part B measured the Ramsey and echo P(1) of the waiting qubits, in Kickoff 47's groups (which add 125), at Kickoff 47's six waits, at a wait equal to the circuit's own, T_M = 2,612 dt (10.45 µs), and at 16 µs, each circuit twice with 2,000 shots. T_M is the time from the end of M's first H to the start of its last, in the circuit without refocusing as scheduled on the live backend: 2,611 dt on every chain, made even. One job of 104 circuits, under IBM's calibration stamp of 01:35 UTC.

    The rule, fixed before the run (according to the author's dated records): for each chain, F_pred = F_ref × (1 − ΔP_R(T_M)), where F_ref is the wait test's F with refocusing and ΔP_R(T_M) is M's Ramsey loss at T_M, the rise in its P(1) over no wait; the residual is d_M = F_no − F_pred, F_no being the F without refocusing, with a 90% interval from a joint bootstrap of both parts. F is the squared Hellinger overlap with the wait test's exact ideal output. The verdict is AGREE if every chain's d_M has its 90% interval inside ±0.15, DISAGREE if any chain's lies entirely outside, and NOT SETTLED otherwise; a chain is informative if its Ramsey loss at T_M has its 90% interval entirely above 0.2, and with no informative chain the verdict would have read "uninformative". The verdict: AGREE, informative, with three informative chains. The rule tests a prediction; it chose no placement.

    - **144-143-142**, with 143 waiting: Ramsey loss at T_M 0.579; F 0.389 without refocusing and 0.915 with; F_pred 0.386; d_M +0.003 (90% interval −0.011 to +0.018). The closest case: 0.389 measured, 0.386 predicted.
    - **141-142-143**, with 142 waiting: Ramsey loss 0.350; F 0.602 and 0.832; F_pred 0.541; d_M +0.060 (+0.041 to +0.079).
    - **125-124-123**, with 124 waiting: Ramsey loss 0.373; F 0.510 and 0.881; F_pred 0.552; d_M −0.042 (−0.063 to −0.022).
    - **143-144-145**, with 144 waiting: Ramsey loss 0.074; F 0.790 and 0.914; F_pred 0.846; d_M −0.057 (−0.068 to −0.046).
    - **132-131-138**, with 131 waiting: Ramsey loss 0.059; F 0.858 and 0.812; F_pred 0.764; d_M +0.094 (+0.081 to +0.109).

    The first three are the informative chains: their Ramsey losses at T_M had 90% intervals entirely above 0.33, and the other two's lay entirely below 0.09.

    Why the prediction should work, an established finding about the circuit, on which the rule rests: in the wait test, M's result should equal the parity of the five end-line results, so when a phase gained by M is the only error, F without refocusing is 1 − P(parity flipped), and that is the probability a Ramsey wait of the same length measures as its loss. Refocusing undoes such a phase, and the rule takes the circuit's other errors from F_ref.

    The wait length matters (a reading after the data). 142 and 124 read NOT REPRODUCED by Kickoff 47's rule at 3.4 µs, with Ramsey losses of 0.06 and 0.07 there, yet they lost 0.35 and 0.37 over the circuit's 10.45 µs wait, and their wait tests lost accordingly: F 0.602 and 0.510 without refocusing, against 0.832 and 0.881 with it. A figure taken at one fixed short wait would have missed them.

## What was known before these findings

That a Ramsey wait (Ramsey, Physical Review 78, 695, 1950) sees a steady frequency offset which a Hahn echo (Hahn, Physical Review 80, 580, 1950) cancels is long established. It is why an echo-measured T2 can miss the loss in findings 13 and 14. Refocusing idle qubits with pulse sequences, dynamical decoupling (Viola, Knill and Lloyd, Physical Review Letters 82, 2417, 1999), is a standard mitigation, and Qiskit provides it. Choosing which idle qubits to refocus has also been studied on IBM processors, because refocusing every idle qubit can lower fidelity: ADAPT (Das, Tannu, Dangwal and Qureshi, MICRO 2021, doi 10.1145/3466752.3480059, arXiv:2109.05309) chooses with decoy circuits whose answer is known. How to insert refocusing for idle qubits of different kinds has been studied too: Niu and Todri-Sanial (arXiv:2204.14251) used Ramsey experiments on IBM's 7-qubit Lagos to explain why one insertion strategy beat another for a qubit beside a two-qubit gate.

A large experiment with mid-circuit resets has also run on ibm_fez. Pokharel, Pan and others (Nature Physics, 2026, doi 10.1038/s41567-026-03470-6) ran adaptive circuits on up to 100 of its qubits, with up to nearly 5,000 measurement-and-reset operations, and chose the qubits by T1, T2, readout and gate errors. Their circuits were estimated to last up to about 2 ms, against a mean T2 of 100 µs on the device (their Supplementary Information). Neither the article nor its supplement describes refocusing during idle time or lists the qubits of each chain, and the article was received on 3 October 2025, a year before these findings. It is cited as context for running reset-heavy circuits on this processor, not as evidence for or against findings 13 and 14. Whether a steady offset on an idle qubit affected their results is not tested here.

These are established findings, cited and not tested here. What findings 13 and 14 add is narrower: on one processor, a qubit whose loss a Ramsey wait showed and IBM's echo-measured T2 did not (finding 13, 10 October), and a rule, fixed before the run, that predicted from one qubit's Ramsey loss at a circuit's own wait length how a reuse-shaped circuit would fare (finding 14, 11 October). Whether that check chooses better than ADAPT, or than refocusing every idle qubit, has not been tested.

## What it has not shown

- **How long a map lasts beyond two days.** On ibm_fez a whole-chip map held for two days (finding 7); on the Rigetti processor a pinned map held for one (finding 9). Neither has been measured over a week. Through Open Quantum, where placement is not recorded, a fixed program's levels held over about 9 hours and a day (Kickoff 37), but its pair names did not locate the physical qubits that ran (finding 6).
- **Why some pairs respond to particular programs on the Rigetti processor.** Kickoff 42 found the response and found that it repeats; it did not identify a cause. Coherent error that depends on the single-qubit frame is the ordinary explanation and is not tested here.
- **Whether choosing qubits by the Ramsey loss at the circuit's wait, or the active-neighbour check, gives better placements for reuse circuits than other methods.** Finding 14 shows the check predicts; it has not been tested as a chooser. The placement proposed after finding 13, compared with Qiskit's, with MERA-style placement, with refocusing every idle qubit and with ADAPT's choice of qubits to refocus, has not been run.
- **Whether the other Kickoff 43 circuits recover with refocusing.** Only XOR_5 was run again with refocusing pulses; BV_10 and Sym_9 also waited in the region on Qiskit's chains.
- **The physical origin of 143's offset.** A steady offset and a frequency that switches slowly between values are not separated (finding 13), and what would cause either was not measured.
- **How long the offset persists.** Finding 13 saw it on one morning. On 11 October 2026, finding 14 fitted it again, about 15 hours later and across one day boundary, and the chain with 143 waiting in it had lost without refocusing under every calibration stamp the runs ran under, over about 22 hours. Finding 15 saw it again on 13 October 2026, REFOCUSABLE at 3.4 µs, about 2.6 days after finding 14's calibration stamp. Whether it lasts longer, and why it is there, are not known; 144's large wait loss was gone within four hours.
- **Other chips.** Findings 11 to 14 are from ibm_fez alone.
- **The active-neighbour check has run on one processor.** It has been tested on six waiting qubits of ibm_fez, 143 and 144 (findings 16 to 18) and four on new chains (finding 19), with one day on new chains. A second day on this code is planned.
- **Its positive lean is unexplained.** The active-neighbour residual d1 has read high in 27 of its 28 cells so far (findings 16 to 19), by up to +0.114. A chain with a larger lean could fail the ±0.15 margin without any offset.
- **It has not been tested as a chooser.** Neither check has been shown to choose better placements. The placement bullet above, on Qiskit's placement, MERA-style placement, refocusing every idle qubit and ADAPT's choice, stands.
- **The mechanism of the neighbour shift is not established.** A static ZZ coupling is consistent with the data (findings 16 to 18) and was not tested as such.
- **The MERA-style comparison.** Kickoff 43's brief required it before any claim about placing reuse circuits by the map; it has not been run.
- **Whether the kept share chooses better pairs on the Rigetti processor.** Twice NOT SETTLED (findings 8 and 9). The plain level did, both times. On both IBM chips the level was scored as a chooser for reference and gained less than the kept share did; no run has yet compared the two scores against each other under a rule naming either as the primary test.

What would discredit the map: a NOISE verdict (it does not repeat), a REDUNDANT verdict (the published figures already carry it), NOT USEFUL (it picks no better pairs), or FADES (it does not last long enough to plan by).
