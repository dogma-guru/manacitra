# Test 11: the rule, fixed before the run

Written 17 October 2026, before any job was built or submitted. Author: Anish Patel. This file states the design, the analysis and the verdict rule for test 11 on ibm_fez qubit 143. Its SHA-256 is posted publicly as an issue before any job is submitted, together with the digests of a desk model's predictions written before this rule, and the file is published unchanged after the run. The verdict rule of the monitor jobs is checked on the desk, on simulated traces of 143, before the hash and again by the runner after it; both checks are published with the run.

## The question

Test 7 (finding 28) found that qubit 143 on ibm_fez carries two fixed frequencies about 90 kHz apart at unequal weight, and the provider's execution record, read afterwards, placed the one change of that weight within the job at the boundary between the first and second execution spans (amendment A26: weights 0.457, 0.315 and 0.274 over the three spans). Test 10 on ibm_kingston then showed that, on a one-second clock at a 2.4 kHz floor, no qubit's phase moved within a span and one of 123 moved at a boundary. A desk model built on tests 7 to 10 (Model 01, version 2) describes the records with four channels, within a span, at a span boundary for frequency, at a span boundary for the weight of a two-line qubit, and across a calibration stamp, and has written its bets for this test before this rule was written. The two bets that this test can settle are on 143: that its frequency does not move within a span or at a span boundary at the monitor's own floor, and that its weight moves across a span boundary in a shuffled job with a probability the model puts between one half and nine in ten.

On ibm_fez, on one day, on qubit 143: under test 7's rule on a second day, is 143 still two fixed frequencies, at what split and weight; with one wait at which its two lines are in antiphase, read in two quadratures and repeated in fixed order every 0.6 s through two jobs, does its phase or its weight change within an execution span, only at the boundaries, or not at all; and what does the record say at each of the model's bets?

## The code

The test 7 fit is `ramsey_xy_fit` of the public branch `findings/t7-two-frequencies` at commit `b7313bbb8839cafcd215434ff18dc329282f06ca`, or the same function at the v0.8.0 release if it exists by the run; the commit used is recorded. The monitor circuits, the per-repeat phase and weight, the switch test and the verdicts are the run's own script, published with the data and checked on the desk as the last section says. **The result's execution metadata (`execution_spans`, and everything else under the result's `metadata`) is saved with the counts at fetch for every job; a job whose metadata cannot be saved is recorded as such and its spans are retrieved again before analysis.**

## The run: three jobs on ibm_fez, qubit 143, in this order

Settings for every job: dynamical decoupling, gate twirling and measurement twirling all off; no readout error mitigation. Qubit 143 alone; every other qubit idle in |0⟩. Each job is submitted once; a failed or cancelled job is recorded and not resubmitted, and the verdicts that depend on it are not given.

**Job 1, test 7's design on day 2.** Test 7's rule as written and hashed on 16 October (`t7-rule.md`): 64 waits 0 to 63 µs, X and Y at each, plus the two readout circuits, 130 circuits, each run as 2 copies of 1,500 shots, interleaving seed 11301 (test 7's design with a new seed, so that the shuffle differs from day 1's), the same fits, bootstrap of 2,000 draws with seed 11304, the same verdict. The verdict is read as **CONFIRMED ON DAY 2** if TWO FIXED FREQUENCIES, else **NOT CONFIRMED ON DAY 2**, with δ, b and their intervals beside day 1's (δ 44.7 to 44.9 kHz; b 0.333 to 0.377). The per-span weights and the copies check split by span are computed as amendment A26 computed them for day 1 (the upper line's implied weight, P held at its fit, averaged over the positions passing the lever in each span; the nearest three positions passing the lever on each side of each boundary; Σz² over same-span and cross-span copy pairs).

**Job 2, the monitor.** One wait, **5.6 µs**, fixed here from test 7's split: at 1/(2 × 2δ) with 2δ = 89.6 kHz the two lines are in antiphase, so the fringe's length reads the weight and a change of the dominant line reads as a half turn. The wait is not changed by job 1's result; the wait job 1's split would give is reported beside it. One repeat is two circuits, X (H, delay, H, measure) then Y (the same with S† before the final H), each 1,000 shots. The job is the two readout circuits (measure; X then measure), then 148 repeats in the same order every time, then the two readout circuits again: 300 circuits, submitted in that order and not interleaved. The scheduled wait is recorded and compared with the requested one.

**Job 3, the monitor again,** the same circuits rebuilt, submitted as soon as job 2's result is fetched, so that the gap between the two jobs is of the order of test 10's (130 s); the gap as the provider's execution record gives it is reported.

**Structural checks before each submission, on the circuits as built and again on the backend's snapshot:** every instruction acts on 143; 143 carries exactly the circuit's gate sequence with its scheduled wait; the exact noiseless P(1) is as the circuit's form requires (job 1: test 7's; jobs 2 and 3: 0 for X, 0.5 for Y, 0 and 1 for the readout circuits); the positions are in the rule's order. Any failure stops the run.

**The estimate and the allowance.** Before each job, the account's remaining allowance is read and recorded with the UTC time; the job's estimate is the measured rate per shot of the most recent job of the same design (test 7's for job 1; test 10's job 1 for jobs 2 and 3) times the job's shots, with IBM's formula beside; a job is submitted only if its estimate with the half-again margin is within the remaining allowance. The decision is recorded before each job is built. The backend list the account exposes at build is recorded.

### When

ibm_fez's published stamps for the previous 48 hours are read before building; no job is submitted inside the computed window or while the day's cycle is in progress, as test 7's rule states; if the 48 hours show no pattern or the interval lies outside 20 to 30 hours, that is recorded and the window does not apply. The stamp at build, at each submission, every 5 minutes, and after each fetch is recorded, so that the three jobs' stamps are on the record and any change between them is named.

## The analysis of the monitor jobs (jobs 2 and 3)

1. **The midpoint c** is the mean of the four readout circuits' P(1) (P(1|0) twice and P(1|1) twice).
2. **Per repeat k = 1, ..., 148:** P_X,k and P_Y,k; the vector v_k = (P_X,k − c, P_Y,k − c); its angle θ_k and its length r_k; the angle's standard error s_k = σ_k / r_k with σ_k² = (P_X(1 − P_X) + P_Y(1 − P_Y)) / (2 × 1,000), floored at 0.02 rad; the length's standard error σ_k.
3. **Usable:** the median r_k over the job at least 0.08.
4. **The spans:** from the result's execution metadata, each repeat is assigned the span that ran both of its circuits; a repeat whose two circuits ran in different spans is named and excluded from the switch counts (its θ_k and r_k are still reported).
5. **The unwrapped phase** and **a phase switch** exactly as test 10's rule: a switch between consecutive repeats where the wrapped |θ_(k+1) − θ_k| exceeds the larger of 0.3 rad and 5 √(s_k² + s_(k+1)²); within a span if both repeats ran in the same span, at a boundary if in consecutive spans. At 5.6 µs a radian is 28.4 kHz; on 143 at antiphase the fringe's length is about 0.135 (test 7's fit), so s_k is about 0.12 rad and the five-standard-error term, about 0.8 rad, is the floor, not the 0.3 rad: **a common change of both lines' frequency is seen at about 23 kHz and above, and a change of the dominant line is a half turn, 3.1 rad.** The floor is named as a limit of the design on this qubit. The counts n_within and n_boundary, the within-span time and rate with its 90% interval, the levels and the slopes as in test 10's rule.
6. **The weight per repeat.** With R0 = A e^{−t/τ′} from job 1's P fit at t = 5.6 µs (if job 1 is CONFIRMED ON DAY 2; otherwise from test 7's fit, 0.487 × e^{−5.6/128.3} = 0.466, and the weight series is reported with that caveat), the implied weight of the upper line is w_k = (1 − r_k / R0) / 2 for a repeat whose θ_k is within π/2 of the job's median phase, and w_k = (1 + r_k / R0) / 2 for a repeat a half turn away, with standard error σ_k / (2 R0). The branch assignment follows from day 1's weight below one half (the lower line dominant, the fringe's phase being the lower line's); if day 2's b is above one half the two branches are exchanged and the exchange is recorded.
7. **A weight switch** between consecutive repeats where |w_(k+1) − w_k| exceeds the larger of 0.10 and 5 √(the sum of the two squared standard errors), within a span or at a boundary as for the phase. At 1,000 shots the five-standard-error term is about 0.12, which is the weight floor the design carries and is named as a limit. The counts n_within,w and n_boundary,w.
8. **The weight per span:** the mean of w_k over the repeats of each span with its standard error, beside day 1's per-span weights from A26 and day 2's from job 1.

## The verdicts

**Job 1, H7 on day 2:** CONFIRMED ON DAY 2 or NOT CONFIRMED ON DAY 2, as above.

**Jobs 2 and 3, per job, the phase:**

| verdict | condition |
|---|---|
| **QUIET** | n_within = 0 and n_boundary = 0 |
| **SWITCHES AT BOUNDARIES ONLY** | n_within = 0 and n_boundary ≥ 1 |
| **SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS** | n_within ≥ 1 |

**Jobs 2 and 3, per job, the weight:** the same three words with the weight counts, **WEIGHT QUIET**, **WEIGHT SWITCHES AT BOUNDARIES ONLY**, **WEIGHT SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS**, given only if job 1 is CONFIRMED ON DAY 2 or the job's median r_k is at least 0.08 under test 7's R0; otherwise no weight word, and the series is listed. A job in which the monitor is not usable is NOT USABLE with its numbers.

**The pooled verdicts** over jobs 2 and 3 as test 10's rule pools them (WITHIN if either job, else BOUNDARIES ONLY if either, else QUIET), for the phase and for the weight.

**The counts of the run,** as the model's bets are written: the number of phase switches at the four span boundaries of jobs 2 and 3 and within their spans; the number of weight switches at those boundaries and within those spans; whether the phase at the end of job 2 and the start of job 3 differ by more than 5 standard errors (the gap between jobs); the number of weight changes of 0.10 or more across the two span boundaries of job 1, read as A26 read day 1 (the nearest three positions passing the lever on each side); and day 2's split and weight beside day 1's.

## The model's bets, fixed before this rule, and what counts against each

The desk model's predictions file (`predictions-model-01-v2.md` and `.json`, digests posted with this rule's hash) says, for this design: at least one phase switch at the four span boundaries of the two monitor jobs with probability 0.008 (interval 0.001 to 0.026), the predictive count being 0; at least one within-span phase switch in about 186 qubit-seconds with probability 0.002; at least one weight change of 0.10 or more across the two span boundaries of a shuffled job with probability 0.52 (interval 0.35 to 0.69) on every eligible qubit of the records, or 0.91 (0.69 to 0.99) on the three qubits the span check tabulated; and a phase change across the gap between the two monitor jobs at about 1 in 60. The model's file puts this monitor's floor at 8.5 kHz from the 0.3 rad term alone; on 143 the record's own floor is about 23 kHz (the analysis above), and the model's bets are stated at the record's own floor, so they are read at 23 kHz. The model has no within-span weight channel; a weight switch within a span in jobs 2 or 3 is a count the model did not predict and is reported as such. Each count of the run is set beside the model's number; nothing here changes the rule or the model after the data.

## Reported beside the verdicts, not used in them

1. **The execution record of each job:** the spans, their start and stop, their positions and the gaps between them; the repeat period per span; which repeats straddled a span.
2. **The boundary table of jobs 2 and 3:** the wrapped phase difference and the weight difference across each boundary with their standard errors, beside the same differences across a within-span pair of repeats drawn at the same place in each span (the control).
3. **The θ_k, r_k and w_k series in full** for both monitor jobs, the levels found and their separation in radians, and whether the separation is within 0.3 rad of π.
4. **Job 1 beside test 7:** both fits, both held-out errors, all intervals; the per-span weights beside A26's; the copies check split by span beside A26's 34.9 on 43 and 836.8 on 85; the telegraph diagnostic and the beat nodes as test 7 reported them.
5. **Readout** from the in-job readout circuits of each job, beside the published value.
6. **The stamps** at build, at each submission, every 5 minutes, and after each fetch; the backend list at build; the estimates, the allowance readings and the decisions.

The rule is not changed after the data, whatever they show.

## The desk check, before the hash

The monitor analysis and verdicts are run on simulated jobs of 148 repeats with binomial shot noise at 1,000 shots, spans of positions 0 to 99, 100 to 199 and 200 to 299, on a qubit with test 7's fitted parameters for 143 (c 0.49; A 0.487; τ′ 128 µs; two lines 89.6 kHz apart; weight 0.355 on the upper line) at the 5.6 µs wait, 100 trials each: (1) steady: QUIET and WEIGHT QUIET at 95% or more, the median w_k within 0.05 of 0.355. (2) The weight stepping from 0.355 to 0.15 at repeat 30 and back at repeat 80, within spans: WEIGHT SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS at 95% or more, both switches found at the planted repeats in 90% or more, the phase QUIET (a weight change on one side of one half does not turn the phase); the same with a step to 0.20, recorded. (3) The weight stepping from 0.355 to 0.645 at the first boundary and back at the second (the dominant line changing): SWITCHES AT BOUNDARIES ONLY at 95% or more for the phase and the weight, the two switches at the boundary repeats and each within 0.5 rad of a half turn in 90% or more (the per-pair standard error of the difference at r 0.135 is about 0.17 rad), and the weight series reading 0.645 in the middle span within 0.05 in 90% or more. (4) Both lines stepping together by 4 kHz at repeat 30 and back at repeat 80: recorded, expected QUIET. (5) The same with a 40 kHz step: SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS at 95% or more, both switches at the planted repeats in 90% or more; the same with 30 kHz and 12 kHz steps, recorded. (6) The weight drawn fresh per shot at 0.5: NOT USABLE or QUIET, recorded. The thresholds (0.3 rad, 0.10 in weight, five standard errors, the 0.08 floor on r, the 0.02 rad floor on s) are kept only if every required rate is met; a change before the hash is recorded in this section with its reason.

Mantri's check before the hash (17 October, 100 trials per case, binomial noise and exponential decay only, which is a limit of the check): every required rate met at 100 of 100, except that case 2's step to 0.20 found both switches at their repeats in 91 of 100 (the planted change, 0.155, sits just above the weight floor of about 0.12) and case 3's half-turn test was first written at 0.3 rad and met in 84 of 100, which is the per-pair noise and not the rule, so the desk-check criterion was widened to 0.5 rad before the hash (met in 100 of 100); the rule's thresholds did not change. The common-frequency floor measured in the check is 0.82 rad, 23 kHz: a 40 kHz step was found at both planted repeats in 100 of 100, a 30 kHz step read SWITCHES WITHIN SPANS in 98 of 100 with both switches placed in 79, a 12 kHz step read QUIET in 99 of 100, a 4 kHz step QUIET in 100 of 100. The weight floor measured is 0.118. The weight flip at the boundaries read SWITCHES AT BOUNDARIES ONLY on both channels in 100 of 100 with the middle span's weight within 0.05 of 0.645 in 100 of 100. Case 6 read NOT USABLE (median r 0.019).
