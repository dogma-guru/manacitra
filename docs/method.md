# The idea in six pictures

This page explains the method in six pictures, then the wait test, the two-quadrature Ramsey, the monitor and its execution spans, and the per-shot read. It is for readers who want to know how a number is made before reading the findings. Read the [README](../README.md) first; every rule and threshold is in [the long version](index.md).

## 1. The kept share

![Two circuits on one pair of qubits, identical except for their single-qubit gates, shown as two rows of boxes joined by the same three CZ gates. Beside them, three pairs of dots on a P(11) axis: the exact model's outcomes, 0.962 without the offset and 1.000 with it, a gap of 0.038; ibm_fez pair 106-107, 0.923 and 0.963, a gap of 0.040, k = 1.06; ibm_fez pair 20-21, 0.824 and 0.833, a gap of 0.008, k = 0.22.](diagrams/kept-share.svg)

The two circuits encode a four-site Hamiltonian in two qubits; the offset makes the transfer from 00 to 11 exact, so the model's P(11) moves from 0.962 to 1.000. On ibm_fez on 5 October 2026, pair [106, 107] moved by 0.040, slightly more than the model's 0.038 (k = 1.06). Pair [20, 21] moved by 0.008 (k = 0.22): the same gates, the same difference in angles, and most of the difference was gone. Both pairs ran in the same job, with 32,000 shots per variant each. (A PNG version of every diagram sits beside the SVG in `docs/diagrams/`.)

## 3. The three checks

![Three scatter plots of ibm_fez's 27 pairs from Kickoff 31, each answering one question of the map rule, with its correlation and threshold above it. 1, does it repeat: k from one half of the runs against k from the other half, beside a dashed line of equal values; r = 0.87, needs at least 0.5, passes. 2, does it carry over: k on circuit A against k on circuit B; r = 0.82, p below 1 in 10,000, needs at least 0.4 with p below 0.05, passes. 3, is it already in the published score: k against IBM's published error score x; r = −0.18, needs |r| below 0.5, and the A-to-B correlation with x removed is 0.82, needs at least 0.3, passes. The title gives the verdict: DIAGNOSTIC.](diagrams/three-checks.svg)

The verdict comes from three checks on the same map. It must repeat: each half of the runs, 16,000 shots per variant per pair, gives nearly the same k (r = 0.87). It must carry over to a second circuit with a different gap (r = 0.82). And it must not be what the published score already says: k barely follows x (r = −0.18), and the carry-over survives with x taken out (r = 0.82). All three pass on ibm_fez, so the verdict is DIAGNOSTIC; a map that fails the first is NOISE, and one that x explains is REDUNDANT.

## 4. The pipeline

![Four boxes in a row joined by arrows: Map (in: 16 short circuits on a set of non-overlapping qubit pairs at once (27 in the published runs); every coupler on a chip can be covered in a few rounds; out: k per pair), Verdict (in: k from two circuits, split halves, published x; out: NOISE, REDUNDANT, DIAGNOSTIC, MAP PRESENT or NOT SETTLED), Pick (in: the map; out: the n pairs whose kept share is closest to the ideal of 1, ranked without checking the verdict), Run, drawn dashed (your own job, run by you on the picked pairs, not by Manacitra). A dashed note above the Map box says that, according to the author's dated records, which are not part of this release, the rules and predictions were sealed before each map job was sent.](diagrams/pipeline.svg)

Map, then verdict, then pick, then run. The verdict says whether the map is worth using at all: a map that does not repeat is NOISE, and a map the published figures already explain is REDUNDANT. Manacitra computes the map and the verdict; `manacitra pick` ranks pairs from the map and does not check the verdict, though it warns when the verdict is not DIAGNOSTIC or MAP PRESENT; and you run your own job. `manacitra pick --by level` ranks by the plain level instead, which chose better pairs on the Rigetti processor where the kept share did not (findings 8 and 9). On an archived file, `pick`, `verdict` and `report` read the counts by the bit reading the file declares (`meta.bit_reading`: IBM's, Open Quantum's or Amazon Braket's), refuse a file that declares none, and give the verdict and statistics of that run's own analysis; a Braket map's published score comes from the figures file it names. In the author's own use, the rules and a set of predictions were written down and hashed before each job was sent, according to the author's dated records, which are not part of this release. From this release on, new commitments can be checked by anyone with `manacitra seal` and, once signing is on, a public timestamp; the runs published here predate this release and cannot be checked that way.

Which to rank by: the kept share chose better pairs on ibm_fez and was not settled on ibm_kingston; the plain level, each pair's mean P(11) on circuit A without the offset, chose better pairs on the Rigetti processor, on the same day and a day later, where the kept share did not (Kickoffs 33, 40 and 41). On every payoff run both scores were scored against the published figures: on IBM the kept share was the primary test and the level a reference line; on Rigetti the reverse; no run has yet compared the two scores against each other under a rule fixed in advance. The dead-pair filter uses the run's own levels, never a list from an earlier day: on the Rigetti processor, dead pairs came and went between consecutive days (18-19 came back; 72-73 and 76-77 went).

Distance from the ideal (Amendment A15). k = 1 is the ideal: a pair that kept exactly the circuit's gap. `manacitra pick` ranks the kept share by its distance from it, |1 − k|, smallest first, after the dead-pair filter. A k well above 1 marks a pair whose two circuits are being pulled apart by an error, not one that kept more, as Kickoff 35's whole-chip maps of ibm_fez show: on Days 2 and 3 the usable pair with the highest k was 33-34, at k 4.52 and 4.66, with a plain level of 0.51 and 0.52. The published Kickoff 33 pick was made highest first, from a prior map whose largest k is 1.07; in that range the two rules differ by one pair (106-107 under highest first, 22-23 under |1 − k|), whose distances from 1 differ by 0.003 against a shot noise of about 0.05 in each. `--by kept-share-highest` reproduces Kickoff 33's rule.

## 6. How to check a sealed prediction

![A timeline of four steps. 1, seal and post the hash (manacitra seal): shows the file existed then, without showing what it says. 2, run the job: the file stays as it was. 3, reveal the salt (manacitra reveal): publishes the file and its salt. 4, anyone verifies (manacitra verify): a match shows the predictions came before the results.](diagrams/sealed-prediction.svg)

This one is for a stranger who wants to know whether the predictions came before the results. `manacitra seal` commits to a file with a salted SHA-256 hash, which is posted somewhere the producer cannot edit; after the run, `manacitra reveal` publishes the salt, and `manacitra verify` lets anyone check that the file is the one committed to. Once the repository is public, a CI workflow also signs every commit record and every data file with Sigstore's keyless signing, which records who signed and when in a public log ([`docs/index.md`](index.md#9-sealing-and-signing)).

```bash
manacitra seal predictions.md      # before the run: prints the hash to post; the salt stays in .seals/
manacitra reveal predictions.md    # after the run: writes the salt into predictions.md.commit.json
manacitra verify predictions.md    # anyone, with the file and its commit record: MATCH
printf 'x' >> predictions.md && manacitra verify predictions.md   # one byte changed: NO MATCH, exit status 1
```

The commit record shows that the file existed when its hash was posted; it does not show when that was. The time
comes only from where the hash was posted (the record's own `sealed_utc` is the sealing machine's clock).

## The wait test

The wait test (`manacitra.wait.build_wait_test`, Amendment A18) asks one question of a chain L1-M-L3 that you name: does the middle line M keep its phase while it waits for its neighbours to be measured and reset? M is put in superposition and waits through three retirements. Each time, a logical qubit on an end line meets M by one CZ and is measured, and its line is reset and taken by the next logical qubit, while M waits as long as that measure plus reset on the backend. Five logical qubits share the two end lines; M's result is the parity of theirs, so the exact output is uniform over 32 of the 64 outcomes, and F, the squared Hellinger overlap with it, is 1 without noise. A phase φ that M gains over its waits takes F to cos²(φ/2): an RZ(π/2) in each wait, or in only one, gives 0.5. The test runs without and with XY4 refocusing pulses, inserted as in Kickoff 47, so a loss that refocusing removes shows as a gain. It reads only the total phase: a whole number of turns (an offset of one cycle per total wait, about 105 kHz with the offline ibm_fez snapshot's durations) leaves F at 1, and an offset whose sign switches from shot to shot looks the same as a steady one. Part B's Ramsey and echo curves, in the same job, read the loss wait by wait. It is a circuit written here, not a published one, and it does not stand in for XOR_5. It ran on five chains of ibm_fez on 11 October 2026, with Part B at the circuit's own wait ([finding 14](findings.md#finding-14)). How to run it, and what a rerun is: [`docs/reproduce.md`](reproduce.md).

### The Ramsey over the circuit's wait

The check of findings 14 to 19 predicts the wait test from the waiting qubit's Ramsey loss over the circuit's own wait, T_M, the time from the end of M's first H to the start of its last as scheduled: F_pred = F_ref × (1 − loss), F_ref being the wait test's F with refocusing (`manacitra.wait.predicted_fidelity`). `manacitra.wait.build_wait_ramsey` builds the Ramsey. By default it is the active-neighbour Ramsey (Amendment A22): the wait test as scheduled, with every two-qubit gate that touches M replaced by a delay of the same length on both its qubits, so that M does only H, wait, H, measure while its neighbours do everything they do in the circuit, at the same times.

Why the neighbours: a Ramsey with them idle in |0⟩ does not see what they do. Held in |1⟩ during the wait, they changed the waiting qubit's loss by 0.25 to 0.35 ([finding 16](findings.md#finding-16)). With a known phase added, the wait test answered as if its offset differed from an idle-neighbour Ramsey's, by about 7° and 22° on two qubits, with the active-neighbour Ramsey closer on both ([findings 17](findings.md#finding-17) and [18](findings.md#finding-18)). On four new chains, the check with the active-neighbour Ramsey held within ±0.15, where the one with idle neighbours would have failed ([finding 19](findings.md#finding-19)). It is the instrument that held in the cases tested, on six waiting qubits of one processor (through finding 19; findings 20 and 21 below). It does not match the circuit exactly, and its residual has read high so far.

The margin's record, on two processors: on ibm_fez the check with the active-neighbour Ramsey predicted the wait test within ±0.15 on every informative cell on two days, across a maintenance ([findings 19](findings.md#finding-19) and [21](findings.md#finding-21)); on ibm_kingston it missed on one informative cell of four, by +0.257, where the check with idle neighbours would have missed on all four ([finding 20](findings.md#finding-20)). The prediction carries the residual's positive lean and any offset left over between the circuit and the Ramsey; on ibm_kingston the lean was larger, and on the qubit that missed, the leftover was estimated at tens of degrees from a single phase.

To choose the 0.5.0 instrument, pass `neighbours="idle"`: Part B's Ramsey on M alone at T_M made even, the instrument of findings 13 and 14, unchanged. Both kinds take a known phase, `phase`, one RZ on M right after its first H, as Kickoffs 51 to 53 used it, and so does `build_wait_test`.

### The check at the peak phase

A known phase φ on the waiting qubit, one RZ right after its first H, moves the Ramsey's loss and the wait test's F along sinusoids, and each sits at its own offset: the Ramsey's offset θ_R1 and the wait test's θ_W. The two need not coincide. Their difference, the leftover θ_W − θ_R1, was −36.7° on one ibm_kingston qubit and 0.0° on another ([finding 22](findings.md#finding-22)). A difference between two shifted sinusoids is largest on their slopes and smallest at their peaks, so at the Ramsey's loss maximum a leftover enters the check only at second order (the reasoning of [finding 23](findings.md#finding-23)'s rule). The check at the peak phase is read there, in two jobs (Amendment A24):

1. **Job 1, the sweep** (`manacitra.wait.phase_sweep`): the active-neighbour Ramsey at φ = 0°, 60°, 120°, 180°, 240° and 300°. With `wait_test=True`, the wait test at the same phases follows, which measures the leftover.
2. **The peak** (`peak_check`, from job 1's counts): M's P(1) at the six phases, the copies pooled; the fit y(φ) = c + b cos φ + s sin φ by least squares (`sweep_fit`); the Ramsey's offset θ_R1 = atan2(s, −b); and φ* = 180° − θ_R1, wrapped to [0°, 360°) and rounded to the nearest degree, halves up (`peak_phase`), from the point estimate. Record φ* before job 2 is built: it is fixed by job 1, not chosen after job 2.
3. **Job 2, the check** (`peak_check`'s circuits): the wait test without refocusing and the Ramsey at φ*, the wait test with refocusing and M's zero-wait baseline, with 0.6.0's check at +90° beside it. Each cell's prediction is `predicted_fidelity`, and finding 19's rule decides.

A cell at φ* sits where the Ramsey's loss is greatest, so it is informative whenever any phase would be: informative by construction, a property of the design. The check at +90° needs no sweep and remains the cheaper form for a chain whose leftover is small; on ibm_kingston, at +90°, it missed on the chain whose leftover was large ([findings 20](findings.md#finding-20), [22](findings.md#finding-22) and [24](findings.md#finding-24)). At the peak phase it predicted the wait test within ±0.15 on twelve of twelve cells over three runs on new chains, on two processors ([findings 23](findings.md#finding-23), [25](findings.md#finding-25) and [27](findings.md#finding-27)); its residual was not smaller than at +90° on every chain, and its positive lean remains.

One shot count per job: qiskit-ibm-runtime 0.49.0 deprecates different shot counts across the circuits of one job (Kickoff 62's job 2 held two and ran, with a warning). Put circuits that need another shot count, such as readout circuits, in a job of their own.

**The readout survey** (`readout_survey`, `readout_errors`). S0 measures every qubit of the backend with no gate and S1 puts one X on each, then the same measures, qubit i into classical bit i; each qubit's readout error is e = (P(1|0) + P(0|1)) / 2, the average that IBM's published readout error is defined as. On ibm_fez on 15 October 2026 the published table did not describe the readout measured in jobs ([findings 26](findings.md#finding-26) and [27](findings.md#finding-27)), so Kickoff 62 screened its draw on the survey. The threshold of any screen is the caller's; Kickoff 62's was 3%.

**The draw, as the kickoffs made it** (a procedure of the runs, not part of the package). Every chain L1-M-L3 with a CZ on both couplers of the live target and L1 ≠ L3 is a candidate. A chain is set aside if it uses a qubit of earlier runs, a qubit not operational, a qubit with a readout error above 3% (published, or from the survey) or a coupler with a CZ error above 1%, each chain counted under the first it fails. The rest are sorted by (M, L1, L3), shuffled with a seed fixed in the rule, and taken in order, skipping any that shares a qubit with one taken, until four are taken. There is no redraw, and a draw that leaves fewer than four stops the run (Kickoff 60, beside [finding 26](findings.md#finding-26)).

### The two-quadrature Ramsey and the two-model fit

A Ramsey fringe read at a few waits on one quadrature can be fitted as one line with decay or as two fixed lines, and a short span does not tell the two apart. The two-quadrature Ramsey reads the fringe at every wait of a long span, in two quadratures, and scores the two models on waits they were not fitted to (Amendment A25; [finding 28](findings.md#finding-28)):

1. **The circuits** (`manacitra.wait.ramsey_xy`): on one qubit, at each wait, X, Part B's Ramsey (H, the wait, H, measure), and Y, the same with an S† before the final H, which advances the fringe by a quarter turn, so that the pair reads the fringe's phase as well as its size. Each wait is made even in the backend's dt, and the wait as scheduled is recorded with each circuit. The two readout circuits on the qubit, a measure and X then a measure, can run in the same job.
2. **The fit and the held-out error** (`ramsey_xy_fit`): two models, fitted jointly over both quadratures by weighted least squares, each point weighted by its binomial standard error. S is one line with decay; P is two fixed lines at Δ0 ± δ under one decay, with a free weight b on the upper line. The caller names the fitted waits, test 7's being the even ones, and each model is scored by its held-out error, the mean absolute difference between its prediction and the measured P(1) at the waits held out. A model that matches the fitted waits by accident does not predict the others.
3. **The band** (`ramsey_xy_band`). Waits a spacing s apart fix a line's frequency only modulo 1/s: on even waits 2 µs apart, a line at f and one at f + 500 kHz are the same function at every fitted wait and differ at every held-out one. P's lines are held inside the band the fitted waits can tell apart, |Δ0| + δ at most 1/(2s), which is computed from their spacing.
4. **The intervals:** a bootstrap that draws each fitted circuit's copies with replacement, redraws each drawn copy's shots and refits P from its point fit; the 5th and 95th percentiles over the refits give each parameter's 90% interval. A rule's thresholds on the held-out errors, on δ and on b are the caller's, and the function says which clauses on them hold; the package fixes none of them.
5. **The copies check** (`copies_check`): for each circuit, the difference of its two copies' P(1) over its binomial standard error, z, and the sum of z² over the circuits against its degrees of freedom. Copies drawn from one binomial give a sum near its degrees of freedom. A fringe that moved during the job does not, and then the bootstrap's intervals describe the job's average. A provider may execute one job as several spans, each a run of the submitted circuits; the span record is retrieved from the result's metadata and archived with the data from finding 28 on. Where the record allows, the copies check is reported split by whether a circuit's two copies ran in the same span.

The one-quadrature fit beside Part B, `ramsey_fit`, is unchanged.

**The desk power check before a rule.** Before a design's rule is fixed and hashed, the design and the rule are run on simulated qubits, with the shot noise the job will have, across the values the run might meet; the rule is kept only if it returns the verdict that generated the data often enough. The keep criterion says how often: for test 7, the generating verdict in at least 80% of 200 draws at three quarters of the counted points of a grid of 135 simulated qubits ([the record](../data/desk/t7-power-check/README.md)).

### The monitor and the execution spans

A shuffled job averages a fringe over its whole run. The monitor reads it at one wait, in a fixed order of repeats, as often as the job allows (Amendment A27; [finding 29](findings.md#finding-29)):

1. **The circuits** (`manacitra.wait.ramsey_monitor`): every qubit of a set at its own wait in one circuit, X and Y as the two-quadrature Ramsey builds them, each qubit measured into its own classical bit, so that the circuit lasts as long as its longest-wait qubit's sequence; and the two readout circuits on every qubit. A job is the readout circuits, a number of repeats of X then Y, and the readout circuits again, in that fixed order as submitted, the positions not shuffled, and the result's execution record is saved at the fetch.
2. **The phase and the length** (`monitor_phase`): from each repeat's two P(1) and the readout circuits' midpoint, the fringe's phase θ_k and length r_k, with their standard errors, floored where the caller says. Consecutive repeats are compared by the wrapped difference of their phases; the caller's thresholds name a switch, between adjacent repeats of a span or across a boundary between spans, and the time the tested pairs cover comes from the span record.
3. **The weight at antiphase** (`monitor_weight`). At the wait 1/(4δ) two fixed lines point in opposite directions, so the fringe's length is R0 |1 − 2w|, R0 the length one line would have there, from a fit. Each repeat's weight w_k follows from r_k and R0, on the branch its phase gives, with the same pairs and the caller's threshold.

**Reading a job by execution span** (`span_assignment`). A provider may execute one job as several spans, each a run of the submitted circuits, with gaps between them; its record gives each span's positions, start and stop. A position belongs to the span the record places it in, whatever its number, and a repeat whose circuits ran in different spans straddles. A26 read finding 28's job this way after the data; from tests 10 and 11 on, the record is saved with the counts, and a design reads its quantities per span: the weight per span, the copies check split by span, and the changes between adjacent repeats of a span set apart from those across its boundaries. Where the shots of a span are read as shared across its circuits (below), adjacent repeats of one span average, on that reading, the same stretch of time, and a change between them cannot appear whatever the qubit did, beyond shot noise.

**Reading the shots of a span** (tests 13 and 15; [finding 29](findings.md#finding-29)). A provider returns, for a completed job, each position's shots one by one. Take a span's positions of one kind, one row per repeat and one column per shot index as returned: if the column means vary far more than independent shots allow (the variance of the column means over the mean of p(1 − p) divided by the rows, above 3), the span's circuits are dependent at equal returned shot index, which the reads take as a state they share, and the series of column means, one per returned index, is on that reading the state's series in returned order, and in seconds only if the returned indices are chronological sweeps evenly spaced across the span. Its correlation lag is where its autocorrelation falls below 1/e of its excess over shot noise, and a span's duty is the fraction b of its returned indices in the high state. Test 15 gives the estimated sampling standard deviation of the duty under a stationary two-state model with exponential correlation, τ_c taken at the 1/e crossing, as √(2 b(1 − b) τ_c / T) for a span of duration T (`telegraph_sd` in its record), not the shots' binomial error; its estimand is the underlying duty. A level difference between spans is read against that error. The reading is on the order the provider returns; it names no cause for the sharing.

**A desk model as a step** (`data/desk/sandhi/`). A kinetic description fits rates of change to the records: per channel, the events counted against the trials or the time each record can see, at the record's own floor, a record below its floor being censored and not counted as nothing. A channel's floor must be the error of what the record can see: Sandhi's version 3 read levels per span at the shots' error and counted switches within spans that a shared state cannot show, and is set down for that (ruling R2 of A27). It describes; it names no cause. Its bets for the next run are written from the fitted rates alone and hashed before that run, so the run can score them, and they are the first test the model has not seen.

### Reading a span per shot

A provider returns, for a completed job, each position's shots one by one. Where a span's circuits are dependent at equal returned shot index, the span's weight is an average over the span, and its error is that of a time average, not the shots' (Amendment A28; [finding 30](findings.md#finding-30); the reads of tests 13, 15, 16 and 17). The package reads a span in four steps:

1. **The sweep series** (`manacitra.wait.sweep_series`). From one qubit's shots per position, keep the positions of one circuit (a quadrature) in the repeats that ran whole in the span, as `span_assignment` decides; build the bit matrix M, one row per repeat and one column per returned shot index; its column means m_j are the sweep series.
2. **The sharing ratio** (`sharing_ratio`). The variance of m_j over the share independent shots would give it, the mean of p_i(1 − p_i) over the rows divided by their number. Near 1 the rows' shots at equal index are independent; far above it the reads take them as sharing a state.
3. **The two-state reading** (`two_state_reading`). The threshold midway between two percentiles of m_j, by default the 10th and the 90th; the duty b, the fraction of the series above it, never folded; the correlation lag, the first lag at which the autocorrelation of m_j falls below 1/e of its excess over the shot-noise share, and the correlation time τ_c, that lag times the caller's sweep time; the switches between the states, the mean dwells, the duties of the span's four quarters, and the mid-sweep fraction, the share of the series in the middle fifth between the two percentiles.
4. **The error of a time average** (`time_average_sd`). The estimated sampling standard deviation of a stationary two-state process's duty, with exponential correlation, over a span of duration T: √(2 b(1 − b) τ_c / T), with a lag under three sweeps read at three, as a ceiling. Two spans are compared by the difference of their duties over their combined standard deviation (`boundary_ratio`).

This is the long-duration form. For a span of duration T, the variance of the time average of a stationary two-state process with exponential correlation is 2 b(1 − b)[τ_c/T − (τ_c/T)²(1 − e^(−T/τ_c))]; on the spans of finding 29 the exact sd is smaller by less than 2.6 percent, the most at test 14's antiphase wait, and on those of finding 30 by less than 1.6 percent. The three-sweep ceiling is an operational floor on the lag, not a confidence bound on τ_c.

For a shared state the shots of a span are not independent looks at the duty: the span holds about T/(2τ_c) independent looks, tens where the shots number tens of thousands, so the shots' binomial error is far too small for a span's weight, and the time average's error replaces it. The times are on the sweep clock, the returned order, and in seconds only if that order is chronological and even. The words for a ratio, their thresholds and the branch that maps a duty to a weight are the caller's; the verdict words stay in the findings.

### A test fixed after a pattern is noticed

A pattern seen in records already published can suggest a test, and the records that suggested it cannot be the test's data. Test 17's middle test is the worked case ([finding 30](findings.md#finding-30)). On 19 October 2026 a working read noticed that, in all four of 143's 1,000-shot monitor jobs, the middle span's duty stood above the mean of the end spans (`data/ibm_fez/t17-middle-note-excerpt.md`). The pattern was written down as a test before any data for it existed: in each new job of three or more spans, the contrast of the spans whose centres lie in the middle third of the job's time against the others, over its error; the jobs pooled into one statistic; the words and their thresholds; its power at the size noticed; and a desk check of its false-alarm rate under no effect. The rule carrying the test was hashed and posted in a public issue, and then run on new jobs only. The rule also said what each outcome would and would not show: a result that does not reach its threshold counts against the pattern at the size noticed, and says little about smaller ones.
